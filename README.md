# AccessCare AI — The Adaptive Digital Clinic

An AI-assisted communication platform that helps patients who find it hard to speak, type or explain symptoms tell a remote doctor what they mean. It does **not** diagnose.

## Core
| Part | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Patient Intent Reconstruction Engine** | Builds a provisional, item-by-item model of what the patient reported. Every item records its source (selected / typed / answered / suggested-and-confirmed / corrected), its first value and every change. It detects missing information and mismatches (pattern vs words, left vs right, severity vs words, onset vs words, “not sure” vs a named sensation), asks one adaptive question at a time and never repeats one. The patient corrects, removes, restores or marks items unsure. A verification record counts what was captured, corrected, resolved and still missing. | `src/services/intent/ReconstructionEngine.ts` |
| **AI Clinical Brief** | 11 sections (main concern … patient corrections). Every line is labeled Patient / AccessCare wording / AI wording / Unknown; clinician notes are kept separate. The patient approves, edits or rejects it. | same + `src/screens/shared/Reconstruction.tsx` |
| **AI assistance (real)** | Inside the Claude app, through the platform `sample` capability (the viewer's own Claude account), **only if the patient opts in**: grounded suggestions from the patient's words, main-concern wording, Silent Signal message wording, a supplementary safety hint. Guards reject any output that adds numbers, body parts, sides, causes, diagnoses or advice, and any suggestion whose quote isn't in the patient's text. If AI is unavailable or declined, rule-based wording is used. | `src/services/ai/AIAssist.ts` |
| **Silent Signal AI** | Its own tab. Methods: large buttons, symbol board, Yes/No one at a time, single-switch scanning (on-screen switch, Space or Enter, so adaptive switches that act as keys work), and speech where the device allows the microphone. Calibration uses non-medical practice targets with confirmation and correction. Ambiguous inputs (two taps at once, a press right as the highlight moves, an unclear spoken match) are never accepted silently. Messages are built from confirmed selections, then reviewed, corrected and approved before they are sent. | `src/services/signal/`, `src/screens/signal/` |
| **Silent Signal camera · sign language** | The camera section now takes answers in **sign language** instead of eye/head movements, in the same place and workflow (setup → sample questions → medical questions one at a time → confirm every answer → summary → approve → doctor). MediaPipe **Hand Landmarker** (vendored, runs in the browser) tracks up to two hands. Signs recognized: (1) **built-in ASL YES** (S-hand/fist nodding) and **ASL NO** (index + middle fingers snapping onto the thumb), detected by rules written from the dictionary form of each sign (`SignCommands.ts`); (2) **the patient's own signs** in any sign language (e.g. Egyptian Sign Language or home signs), taught 3 times each and matched by DTW on normalised hand points (`SignRecognizer.ts`). Signs map to the existing commands Yes / No / Next / Choose / Not sure. Each sign is practised (3 attempts) before use; a sign that matches nothing is reported as not recognized, two matching signs as ambiguous — never guessed. **Scope (honest):** only YES and NO are built in, and they are ASL; Arabic/Egyptian sign languages are not built in because no suitable recognition model or dataset was available; it does not translate signed sentences or read faces. Taught examples (hand-point numbers) stay on the device; settings and the mapping are saved with the account. No video is recorded or sent. Old eye/head movement settings are not carried over (the patient sets up signs once). | `src/services/sign/`, `src/screens/signal/CameraSignal.tsx`, `vendor/mediapipe/` |
| **Conversation manager (voice / text)** | The consultation is a conversation, not a fixed questionnaire. One record (`ConsultRecord`) keeps each complaint with the patient's original words, body regions, location detail, side, sensation, strength, onset, sudden/gradual, pattern and associated symptoms; every fact is marked *your words* / *you chose* / *interpretation (confirmed or not)*; “not sure” stays not sure; corrections are logged; contradictions are clarified. The next question is chosen from what is missing for the symptoms the patient actually reported (a headache gets questions about the head and how it started — never a list of unrelated body areas), with contextual answer choices. With AI on, Claude reads the record and the message and returns JSON (understood details with quotes, an empathetic reply in plain English, the next question and choices); AccessCare validates it (allowed values only, quotes checked, interpretations confirmed with the patient, no diagnosis / medicines / false reassurance, safety rules can't be removed). Without AI, or when it fails (said so on screen), English and Arabic (Egyptian and Modern Standard) vocabulary rules do the same job less flexibly, including greetings and “Did you mean…?” for misspelled symptom words. Patients can talk in Arabic or English; the conversation (and the app) follows the language they use. Used in Blind Mode and in **Talk to AccessCare** (home card; speak, type, tap or use the body map, in the same conversation). | `src/services/conversation/`, `src/domain/conversation.ts` |
| **Voice** | Browser speech synthesis with the best installed voice chosen automatically (“enhanced/premium/natural” voices preferred; robotic novelty voices avoided), per-language voice picker, speed, test button, long replies split at sentence ends; pause / resume / stop / repeat, never two voices at once. Optional **natural neural voice** (Azure AI Speech, `en-US-JennyNeural` / `ar-EG-SalmaNeural`, `fr-FR-DeniseNeural`, `ur-PK-UzmaNeural`, `hi-IN-SwaraNeural`; Arabic needs worker version 4, French/Urdu/Hindi version 8) through the worker's `/tts` route when `AZURE_SPEECH_KEY` and `AZURE_SPEECH_REGION` are set; otherwise, or if it fails, the device voice is used and the screen says which voice is speaking. | `src/services/voice/Speech.ts`, `server/cloudflare-worker.mjs` |
| **Blind Access & Voice Companion** | Route `va`, usable signed out and for patients. Entry: the first focusable control on the welcome screen is a labelled Blind Mode button for VoiceOver/TalkBack/keyboard; **three deliberate left swipes** (one finger, fast, mostly horizontal; scrolls, slow drags, two-finger touches and broken sequences reset it) or **Alt+V** also start it; Blind Mode is remembered on the device. Spoken guidance (browser text-to-speech; rate, voice, brief/full) and spoken answers (browser speech recognition after an explicit microphone step that discloses the browser's speech service), with large buttons and typing as alternatives everywhere. Conversation engine `VoiceDialog` (EN + AR words): onboarding (consent → name → password typed, never spoken → **Patient ID** read in groups / slowly → **recovery code** with a privacy warning, save/copy → Doctor ID confirmed by name → AI consent), menu, consultation (the conversation manager above → spoken review → change/approve/reject → confirm send), doctor messages (read, navigate, dictate reply, confirm), doctor and status, settings, log out with confirmation. Repeat / back / help / menu anywhere; skipped = unknown; urgent words (EN/AR) → spoken emergency guidance at once. With AI consent: Claude runs the consultation conversation (validated, see above) and interprets unclear menu answers (read back for a yes). Optional swipe navigation: right/left move between controls and announce them, double tap activates, long press describes, a single tap only selects; customisable mapping; off for screen-reader users. Patient ID `AC-PAT-XXXXXX` is an identifier only; recovery codes are single-use and stored only as a hash (offline) or as a wrapped key (cloud, so encrypted data survives a reset). Settings saved with the account (cross-device in the shared database). No audio is recorded or stored. | `src/services/voice/`, `src/screens/voice/`, `src/domain/voice.ts` |
| **Blind Mode everywhere** | For patients and signed-out visitors (doctor accounts have no Blind Mode button, gestures or shortcut). One controller around every screen (`src/a11y/`). Turn it on from **any** screen with **three quick left swipes** (touch; scrolls, slow drags and swipes on the 3D body, maps or camera never count), **three quick taps on an empty part of the screen**, the **Blind Mode** button in the top bar / side bar, or **Alt+Shift+B**. It stays on while moving between screens and after a reload (remembered on the device), and turns off with **three quick right swipes** (a faster burst than turning on — each within 0.5 s — so moving between controls with single right swipes doesn't turn it off), by voice, button or shortcut. When on: it says which screen is open, asks once for the microphone, then **listens by itself** and carries out **real actions** — open any page of the account’s role, press a control by its name (consequential ones such as sending, connecting a doctor or logging out are confirmed first), read the page or a form, fill a form field by field (passwords are never taken by voice), read the signed-in person’s latest report or the doctor’s latest reply from their own account data, start a spoken symptom report, change the language, go back, repeat, explain, stop, speak slower/faster. On touch screens a tap reads a control and a double tap uses it; swipe right/left moves between controls. Errors, alerts and toasts that appear are spoken. Requests the rules don’t know are matched to the names of the controls on screen, and — only for patients who allowed AI — Claude may interpret them, but it can only choose a page or control that exists (anything else is refused). Microphone problems are spoken and shown; it never claims to listen when it isn’t. Spoken text also goes to a live region, every screen has a main landmark and a heading that receives focus after navigation, so screen readers work with or without it, and without any AI service. | `src/a11y/BlindLayer.tsx`, `commands.ts`, `screenModel.ts` |
| **Five languages: English, Arabic, French, Urdu, Hindi** | Language menu in the top bar / side bar (each language in its own script, remembered on the device, arrow keys and Escape work). Arabic and Urdu turn the interface right-to-left; Urdu uses Noto Nastaliq Urdu and Hindi Noto Sans Devanagari. The interface is written once in English and shown in the chosen language through dictionaries of the app’s own text (`src/i18n/ar.ts`, `fr.ts`, `ur.ts`, `hi.ts`; `dict.ts` also fills in changing parts such as names and numbers) — form values, messages, reports and anything people wrote are never changed; text without an entry stays in English (never machine-guessed at run time). `scripts/i18n-keys.ts` lists every text the app can show or say, and `tests/i18n.test.ts` fails if French, Urdu or Hindi is missing any of them. **Blind Mode, Talk to AccessCare and the voice guide speak and listen in the chosen language** (`fr-FR`, `ur-PK`, `hi-IN`, `ar-EG`/`ar-SA`): the commands (“ouvre les paramètres”, “ترتیبات کھولو”, “सेटिंग्स खोलो”), the voice dialog, the symptom vocabulary with “not” handled (“بخار نہیں ہے”, “मुझे बुखार नहीं है”, “pas de nausées”), urgent words, medicine check-ins, the diary and the visit interpreter (any two of the five languages). The language can be changed by voice (“passe en arabe”, “उर्दू में बदलो”). With AI on, Claude is told to answer in the patient’s language. Emergency numbers are given per language (France 15/112, Pakistan 1122/115, India 112/108, UAE 998/999, Egypt 123). | `src/i18n/`, `src/services/langWords.ts`, `src/services/conversation/lexiconMore.ts` |
| **Design** | One design system (`src/ui/styles.ts`): slate structure, Nile teal for actions, saffron reserved for keyboard focus and Blind Mode; Atkinson Hyperlegible (low-vision legibility) with IBM Plex Sans Arabic; logical CSS properties so RTL mirrors. Phones: top bar (status, language, Blind Mode) + bottom tabs; wide screens: side navigation and a centred reading column. Patients and doctors get their own navigation in the same design. | `src/app/Shell.tsx`, `src/ui/` |
| **Symptom diary (patient) → “What changed?” (doctor)** | New **Diary** section for patients (and a “Today’s check-in” card on Home): say or type how you are in your own words, in Arabic or English (“my headache is better, about 4, but I feel dizzy”). Rules (and Claude, if the patient allowed AI) turn it into items — symptom, strength 0–10, better/same/worse, medicine taken — each marked *your words / you chose / understood from your words*; AI items must quote the patient’s words and use known symptom ids, otherwise they are dropped. The patient checks and edits everything, can have it read aloud, and chooses whether to share each entry with the connected doctor. Urgent words show emergency guidance at once. A chart shows strength over time (with a table version). New **Progress** section for doctors: patients ordered by what needs attention, and per patient a **What changed?** list computed by rules — stronger/eased symptoms, new symptoms, symptoms no longer mentioned, overall feeling, missed medicine, gaps, check-ins since the last report — every line citing its sources (D1… check-ins, C1… reports). Optional AI summary rewords those facts; lines without valid references, with new numbers, or with diagnostic/treatment wording are removed, and only check-ins written while the patient allowed AI are sent. Stored encrypted in the Claude database (vault copy + pair copy only while shared and connected; removed from the doctor on disconnect); `diary_entries` table with RLS for Supabase. | `src/services/diary/`, `src/screens/diary/`, `src/domain/diary.ts` |
| **“Here?” — point and ask** | In the doctor’s consultation messages (**Here?** mode): tap one or more areas on the 3D body / 2D map / list and pick a question (“Does it hurt here?”, numbness, swelling, where it started), optionally asking strength 0–10. The patient sees the same area highlighted on their own body map (home card + Messages), answers Yes / No / Not sure by tap or voice (Arabic or English, e.g. “أيوه حوالي ٧”), checks the answer and approves it; the doctor sees “Left knee: Yes · 7/10”. | `src/screens/shared/Interact.tsx`, `src/domain/interact.ts` |
| **Teach-back (understanding check)** | After the doctor sends a prescription or visit explanation, the patient is invited (home card + Messages) to explain the plan in their own words, by typing or voice, Arabic or English. Rules compare it with what the doctor wrote: which medicine, how much, how often, for how long, and when to get urgent help (from the prescription, or from the explanation when there is no prescription) — each point *understood / different / not mentioned*. With the patient’s AI consent, Claude may judge only points the rules could not find, and only by quoting the patient’s words. The “correct” wording shown is always the doctor’s. The patient can try again, have it read aloud, and approves sending; the doctor sees “1 understood · 2 different” with the patient’s words and the plan side by side. | `src/services/teachback/check.ts` |
| **My care (patient hub)** | New **Care** tab (Home · Care · Signal · Diary · History · Profile; My Doctor is on Home and Profile) with the next dose and six sections below. | `src/screens/care/` |
| **Medicines: schedule, reminders, check-ins, adherence** | The schedule comes **only from prescriptions the doctor approved and sent** (never invented): times from the frequency (“twice daily” → 09:00 · 21:00, “as needed” → no times), the course end from the duration. The patient can change the times (a device setting; the times are also recorded with each check-in so other devices and the doctor count the same way). Each dose: **Taken / Missed / Side effect** by tap, by voice (“I took it”, “خدتها”, “نسيت”, “it made me dizzy”) or from the home card and a reminder banner. Reminders work **while the app is open** (in-app banner, a phone notification if allowed, spoken in Blind Mode) — there are no server push notifications. Check-ins are shared with the connected doctor unless the patient turns sharing off. | `src/services/care/meds.ts`, `src/screens/care/MedScreens.tsx`, `CareSync.tsx` |

## AI in the web version (GitHub Pages)
Outside Claude the page cannot use Claude's `sample` capability, and it must never contain an API key. `server/cloudflare-worker.mjs` is a small Cloudflare Worker that keeps `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` as a secret, accepts only AccessCare's own fixed prompts (prefix allow-list, 12 000-character limit, answers capped at 1 200 tokens, 1 600 for the clinical engine), forwards FDA label lookups by medicine name (`/fda`, fallback when the browser can't reach openFDA directly) from `ALLOWED_ORIGINS`, and returns the JSON answer. Optional `/tts` (Azure AI Speech key kept as a secret) and `/status` (which services exist, no secrets). **Version 8:** natural voices for French, Urdu and Hindi (optional; without it the device voice is used). **Version 7:** `/db` shared storage for the website (bind a D1 database as `DB`; tables are created automatically). **Version 6:** `ALLOWED_ORIGINS` defaults to the GitHub Pages site; a pasted key is cleaned (spaces, quotes, line breaks) and may be saved as `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `CLAUDE_API_KEY` or `API_KEY`; if the model name is unavailable another current model is tried; one photo is accepted only with the medicine-box prompt; the visit-interpreter prompt is allowed. Build the web version with `ACCESSCARE_AI_ENDPOINT=https://<worker>.workers.dev node scripts/build.mjs`. Set a spending limit in the Anthropic console (the origin check stops other websites, not direct calls). Tested: `tests/worker.test.ts` and `tests/e2e/blind_web.py` (the worker runs in the test server against a fake Claude API).

## Not done / limits
- AI calls were tested against a stand-in, not the live Claude model; real wording will vary (the guards still apply).
- Sign language: only ASL YES and NO are built in; their rules were tested on synthetic hand data, not on real signers — they need checking (and probably tuning) with deaf signers. Arabic/Egyptian sign language needs a model trained on a licensed ArSL/ESL dataset; until then patients teach their own signs. No continuous signing or sentence translation. The camera is not available inside Claude's preview (the app says so and offers other methods); use the web build. MediaPipe files come from public Git mirrors (see `vendor/mediapipe/NOTICE.md`) because npm/CDN were unreachable from the build environment — replace with the official package and model, pinned by hash, for production. Speech input is disabled inside Claude's preview.
- Blind Mode: gestures and speech were tested with simulated touch events and speech engines in Chromium. They need checking on real iPhones/Android phones with real voices — mobile browsers and screen readers (VoiceOver/TalkBack) can take over swipes and taps, in which case the Blind Mode button and the keyboard shortcut remain. Listening stops while the app speaks (to avoid hearing itself); tap the screen or press Stop to interrupt it.
- French, Urdu and Hindi: the translations were prepared with AI help and have **not yet been reviewed by native speakers or clinicians** — they should be before real patients rely on them. Speech depends on the device: many phones have French and Hindi voices and recognition, Urdu voices are often missing (then text is shown and a natural voice from worker version 8 can be used). Roman Urdu/Hindi (Latin letters) is only partly understood. Doctor-side clinical-support text and reports stay in the language they were written in.
- Arabic: the interface dictionary covers the patient screens, navigation, accounts, settings and Blind Mode. Doctor-side clinical-support text produced by the rule engine, medical reports and anything people wrote stay in the language they were written in. Egyptian Arabic speech recognition depends on the browser (Chrome supports `ar-EG`; Apple devices use `ar-SA`).
- Natural voice: the Azure route is implemented and tested against a stand-in only; it needs an Azure Speech key to be used.
- No native iOS/Android build. Video calls and server push notifications are not implemented: medicine reminders and doctor alerts work while the app is open (in-app, plus a phone notification if allowed). Keep a normal alarm.
- Medicine-box check: the photo is read by Claude and can be misread; it was tested with a stand-in that is told what the photo shows, not with real boxes. It matches printed names only (a brand name with no generic name printed is not recognised). The camera is not available inside Claude's preview — “Choose a photo” works there.
- Visit interpreter: tested with a stand-in translator; real translations vary. The number check catches changed numbers, not every meaning error. Not a substitute for a professional interpreter.
- Caregivers: the caregiver's view is a summary written when the patient's app is open (not live); caregivers are not available on the Supabase deployment yet.
- Password recovery is impossible by design in cloud mode.
- Clinical engine: warning rules cover a limited set of signs from public NHS pages (not clinical guidelines); the AI parts and openFDA were tested against stand-ins; FDA label text checks are text matches, not a complete interaction database; no licensed drug-interaction service is connected; doctor clinical-support content is English.
- Not clinically validated; no regulatory compliance claimed. The metrics are per-report counts, not accuracy statistics.
