# AccessCare AI — The Adaptive Digital Clinic

An AI-assisted communication platform that helps patients who find it hard to speak, type or explain symptoms tell a remote doctor what they mean. It does **not** diagnose.

## Core
| Part | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Patient Intent Reconstruction Engine** | Builds a provisional, item-by-item model of what the patient reported. Every item records its source (selected / typed / answered / suggested-and-confirmed / corrected), its first value and every change. It detects missing information and mismatches (pattern vs words, left vs right, severity vs words, onset vs words, “not sure” vs a named sensation), asks one adaptive question at a time and never repeats one. The patient corrects, removes, restores or marks items unsure. A verification record counts what was captured, corrected, resolved and still missing. | `src/services/intent/ReconstructionEngine.ts` |
| **AI Clinical Brief** | 11 sections (main concern … patient corrections). Every line is labeled Patient / AccessCare wording / AI wording / Unknown; clinician notes are kept separate. The patient approves, edits or rejects it. | same + `src/screens/shared/Reconstruction.tsx` |
| **AI assistance (real)** | Inside the Claude app, through the platform `sample` capability (the viewer's own Claude account), **only if the patient opts in**: grounded suggestions from the patient's words, main-concern wording, Silent Signal message wording, a supplementary safety hint. Guards reject any output that adds numbers, body parts, sides, causes, diagnoses or advice, and any suggestion whose quote isn't in the patient's text. If AI is unavailable or declined, rule-based wording is used. | `src/services/ai/AIAssist.ts` |
| **Silent Signal AI** | Its own tab. Methods: large buttons, symbol board, Yes/No one at a time, single-switch scanning (on-screen switch, Space or Enter, so adaptive switches that act as keys work), and speech where the device allows the microphone. Calibration uses non-medical practice targets with confirmation and correction. Ambiguous inputs (two taps at once, a press right as the highlight moves, an unclear spoken match) are never accepted silently. Messages are built from confirmed selections, then reviewed, corrected and approved before they are sent. | `src/services/signal/`, `src/screens/signal/` |
| **Silent Signal camera · sign language** | The camera section now takes answers in **sign language** instead of eye/head movements, in the same place and workflow (setup → sample questions → medical questions one at a time → confirm every answer → summary → approve → doctor). MediaPipe **Hand Landmarker** (vendored, runs in the browser) tracks up to two hands. Signs recognized: (1) **built-in ASL YES** (S-hand/fist nodding) and **ASL NO** (index + middle fingers snapping onto the thumb), detected by rules written from the dictionary form of each sign (`SignCommands.ts`); (2) **the patient's own signs** in any sign language (e.g. Egyptian Sign Language or home signs), taught 3 times each and matched by DTW on normalised hand points (`SignRecognizer.ts`). Signs map to the existing commands Yes / No / Next / Choose / Not sure. Each sign is practised (3 attempts) before use; a sign that matches nothing is reported as not recognized, two matching signs as ambiguous — never guessed. **Scope (honest):** only YES and NO are built in, and they are ASL; Arabic/Egyptian sign languages are not built in because no suitable recognition model or dataset was available; it does not translate signed sentences or read faces. Taught examples (hand-point numbers) stay on the device; settings and the mapping are saved with the account. No video is recorded or sent. Old eye/head movement settings are not carried over (the patient sets up signs once). | `src/services/sign/`, `src/screens/signal/CameraSignal.tsx`, `vendor/mediapipe/` |
| **Conversation manager (voice / text)** | The consultation is a conversation, not a fixed questionnaire. One record (`ConsultRecord`) keeps each complaint with the patient's original words, body regions, location detail, side, sensation, strength, onset, sudden/gradual, pattern and associated symptoms; every fact is marked *your words* / *you chose* / *interpretation (confirmed or not)*; “not sure” stays not sure; corrections are logged; contradictions are clarified. The next question is chosen from what is missing for the symptoms the patient actually reported (a headache gets questions about the head and how it started — never a list of unrelated body areas), with contextual answer choices. With AI on, Claude reads the record and the message and returns JSON (understood details with quotes, an empathetic reply in plain English, the next question and choices); AccessCare validates it (allowed values only, quotes checked, interpretations confirmed with the patient, no diagnosis / medicines / false reassurance, safety rules can't be removed). Without AI, or when it fails (said so on screen), English and Arabic (Egyptian and Modern Standard) vocabulary rules do the same job less flexibly, including greetings and “Did you mean…?” for misspelled symptom words. Patients can talk in Arabic or English; the conversation (and the app) follows the language they use. Used in Blind Mode and in **Talk to AccessCare** (home card; speak, type, tap or use the body map, in the same conversation). | `src/services/conversation/`, `src/domain/conversation.ts` |
| **Voice** | Browser speech synthesis with the best installed voice chosen automatically (“enhanced/premium/natural” voices preferred; robotic novelty voices avoided), per-language voice picker, speed, test button, long replies split at sentence ends; pause / resume / stop / repeat, never two voices at once. Optional **natural neural voice** (Azure AI Speech, `en-US-JennyNeural` / `ar-EG-SalmaNeural`; Arabic needs worker version 4) through the worker's `/tts` route when `AZURE_SPEECH_KEY` and `AZURE_SPEECH_REGION` are set; otherwise, or if it fails, the device voice is used and the screen says which voice is speaking. | `src/services/voice/Speech.ts`, `server/cloudflare-worker.mjs` |
| **Blind Access & Voice Companion** | Route `va`, usable signed out and for patients. Entry: the first focusable control on the welcome screen is a labelled Blind Mode button for VoiceOver/TalkBack/keyboard; **three deliberate left swipes** (one finger, fast, mostly horizontal; scrolls, slow drags, two-finger touches and broken sequences reset it) or **Alt+V** also start it; Blind Mode is remembered on the device. Spoken guidance (browser text-to-speech; rate, voice, brief/full) and spoken answers (browser speech recognition after an explicit microphone step that discloses the browser's speech service), with large buttons and typing as alternatives everywhere. Conversation engine `VoiceDialog` (EN + AR words): onboarding (consent → name → password typed, never spoken → **Patient ID** read in groups / slowly → **recovery code** with a privacy warning, save/copy → Doctor ID confirmed by name → AI consent), menu, consultation (the conversation manager above → spoken review → change/approve/reject → confirm send), doctor messages (read, navigate, dictate reply, confirm), doctor and status, settings, log out with confirmation. Repeat / back / help / menu anywhere; skipped = unknown; urgent words (EN/AR) → spoken emergency guidance at once. With AI consent: Claude runs the consultation conversation (validated, see above) and interprets unclear menu answers (read back for a yes). Optional swipe navigation: right/left move between controls and announce them, double tap activates, long press describes, a single tap only selects; customisable mapping; off for screen-reader users. Patient ID `AC-PAT-XXXXXX` is an identifier only; recovery codes are single-use and stored only as a hash (offline) or as a wrapped key (cloud, so encrypted data survives a reset). Settings saved with the account (cross-device in the shared database). No audio is recorded or stored. | `src/services/voice/`, `src/screens/voice/`, `src/domain/voice.ts` |
| **Blind Mode everywhere** | One controller around every screen (`src/a11y/`). Turn it on from **any** screen with **three quick left swipes** (touch; scrolls, slow drags and swipes on the 3D body, maps or camera never count), **three quick taps on an empty part of the screen**, the **Blind Mode** button in the top bar / side bar, or **Alt+Shift+B**. It stays on while moving between screens and after a reload (remembered on the device), and turns off by voice, button or shortcut. When on: it says which screen is open, asks once for the microphone, then **listens by itself** and carries out **real actions** — open any page of the account’s role, press a control by its name (consequential ones such as sending, connecting a doctor or logging out are confirmed first), read the page or a form, fill a form field by field (passwords are never taken by voice), read the signed-in person’s latest report or the doctor’s latest reply from their own account data, start a spoken symptom report, change the language, go back, repeat, explain, stop, speak slower/faster. On touch screens a tap reads a control and a double tap uses it; swipe right/left moves between controls. Errors, alerts and toasts that appear are spoken. Requests the rules don’t know are matched to the names of the controls on screen, and — only for patients who allowed AI — Claude may interpret them, but it can only choose a page or control that exists (anything else is refused). Microphone problems are spoken and shown; it never claims to listen when it isn’t. Spoken text also goes to a live region, every screen has a main landmark and a heading that receives focus after navigation, so screen readers work with or without it, and without any AI service. | `src/a11y/BlindLayer.tsx`, `commands.ts`, `screenModel.ts` |
| **Arabic and English** | Language switch in the top bar / side bar (remembered on the device); Arabic turns the whole interface right-to-left on the same screen. The interface is written once in English and shown in Arabic through a dictionary of the app’s own text (`src/i18n/ar.ts`) — form values, messages, reports and anything people wrote are never changed. Blind Mode, Talk to AccessCare and the voice guide speak and listen in the chosen language (`ar-EG` on Chrome/Android, `ar-SA` on Apple devices, which have no Egyptian recognizer). | `src/i18n/` |
| **Design** | One design system (`src/ui/styles.ts`): slate structure, Nile teal for actions, saffron reserved for keyboard focus and Blind Mode; Atkinson Hyperlegible (low-vision legibility) with IBM Plex Sans Arabic; logical CSS properties so RTL mirrors. Phones: top bar (status, language, Blind Mode) + bottom tabs; wide screens: side navigation and a centred reading column. Patients and doctors get their own navigation in the same design. | `src/app/Shell.tsx`, `src/ui/` |
| **Clarification bridge** | The doctor asks about a specific report item, in a format suggested by the patient's shared preferences (Yes/No, choices, symbols, text). The patient answers with their own method, reviews the wording and approves it. Answers appear in the doctor's brief as “clarified after sharing”. | `src/screens/shared/MessagesPanel.tsx` |
| **Clinical Intelligence Engine (doctor decision support)** | In the doctor's consultation workspace (**Clinical support** tab; the queue lists reports with warning signs first). Built from the report the patient approved and sent, answers the patient approved after sending, and what the doctor documented — every observation cites fact ids (F1…). **Rules (always):** warning signs from public NHS urgent-help pages (stroke/FAST, headache with neurological symptoms, sudden severe headache, fever with stiff neck/rash, chest pain features, breathing difficulty, fainting, sudden/severe stomach pain or GI bleeding, sepsis signs, heavy bleeding, self-harm) — an unanswered item counts as *possibly present* for urgency, never as “no”; missing information chosen per complaint with why it matters, sent to the patient as an accessible clarification with one tap (“asked — awaiting answer”, never asked twice); contradictions. **AI (only if the patient allowed AI):** clinical considerations and next steps that must cite existing facts; probabilities, doses, invented facts and citations outside the checked reference list are rejected (and counted); AI can add warnings, never remove one. **Medication options:** withheld during an emergency warning and until age, allergies and current medicines are documented (and pregnancy where relevant); AI proposes generic names only (no doses); each is checked against its **FDA label via openFDA** (only the medicine name is sent): boxed warning, contraindications, label text mentioning the documented allergies / medicines / age / pregnancy / kidney / liver, DailyMed link; a label-class match to a documented allergy removes the option; options without a label, or when openFDA can't be reached, are not shown. **Prescriptions:** the doctor writes dose and directions, approves explicitly, then sends; it reaches the patient as a doctor-only message type that backends refuse without a complete approval. Doctor decisions (accept / needs assessment / reject), documentation, options, prescriptions and an audit log are stored privately for the doctor (encrypted in the Claude database; `clinical_reviews` table with RLS in Supabase). Not a diagnosis; not clinically validated. | `src/services/clinical/`, `src/domain/clinical.ts`, `src/screens/doctor/ClinicalPanel.tsx` |
| **Follow-up after treatment & visit explanation** | From the doctor's Clinical support tab. **Follow-up:** the doctor schedules it (now / 1 / 3 / 7 days, linked to a sent prescription); when due it appears on the patient's home screen (no push notifications) as large buttons that can be read aloud: how they feel, side effects, taking the medicine, optional words; the patient reviews and approves the answers. Lip/face/tongue/throat swelling or trouble breathing shows emergency guidance immediately (NHS anaphylaxis page); rash or feeling worse → urgent; the doctor sees the flags in the panel and on the dashboard. **Visit explanation:** the doctor writes what the patient should know; if the patient allowed AI, Claude rewords it in plain, simple English — rejected if it adds numbers or reassurance, with warnings if it drops a medicine or urgent-help advice; the doctor must confirm reviewing it before sending. The patient sees it with read aloud. Both travel as doctor-only / patient-only message types through the existing encrypted messages. | `src/screens/shared/FollowUp.tsx`, `src/screens/doctor/FollowUpSections.tsx`, `src/services/clinical/visit.ts` |
| **Safety monitor** | Deterministic red-flag rules run before and during reporting (including “trouble breathing” and “I need help now” in Silent Signal). AI can only add a warning. Whenever no warning pattern is recognized, the app says so without implying the patient is safe. | `src/services/safety.ts` |
| **3D body** | Realistic adult figure built from the **MakeHuman base mesh, macro targets and skin weights (CC0 1.0)** by `scripts/build_body_makehuman.py`: young-adult proportions, one Catmull-Clark subdivision (~57k vertices, 108k triangles, 1.5 MB GLB), voxel ambient occlusion, 32 regions labelled from MakeHuman's bone weights refined with joint positions (elbow/wrist/knee/ankle bands, chest/abdomen and upper/lower back levels, face vs head). WebGL renderer with warm skin shading, front/back/left/right views, rotate, pinch/scroll zoom, double-tap zoom to a region, tap label, clear all; 2D map and list fallbacks. Surface only — no internal anatomy. The previous procedural figure script (`scripts/sculptBody.ts`) is kept for reference. | `src/body/`, `scripts/build_body_makehuman.py`, `vendor/makehuman/` |

## AI in the web version (GitHub Pages)
Outside Claude the page cannot use Claude's `sample` capability, and it must never contain an API key. `server/cloudflare-worker.mjs` is a small Cloudflare Worker that keeps `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` as a secret, accepts only AccessCare's own fixed prompts (prefix allow-list, 12 000-character limit, answers capped at 1 200 tokens, 1 600 for the clinical engine), forwards FDA label lookups by medicine name (`/fda`, fallback when the browser can't reach openFDA directly) from `ALLOWED_ORIGINS`, and returns the JSON answer. Optional `/tts` (Azure AI Speech key kept as a secret) and `/status` (which services exist, no secrets). Build the web version with `ACCESSCARE_AI_ENDPOINT=https://<worker>.workers.dev node scripts/build.mjs`. Set a spending limit in the Anthropic console (the origin check stops other websites, not direct calls). Tested: `tests/worker.test.ts` and `tests/e2e/blind_web.py` (the worker runs in the test server against a fake Claude API).

## Builds
`node scripts/build.mjs` → `dist/accesscare-ai.html` (Claude artifact; publish `dist/mp/*` next to it), `dist/web/` (static site), `dist/accesscare-ai-standalone.html` (one large file with hand tracking embedded; open in Chrome/Edge/Safari/Firefox — runs in offline demo mode unless built with the Supabase variables).

## Running it inside Claude
Publish `dist/accesscare-ai.html` as an artifact (capabilities `db`, `user`, `sample`, `downloads`). Inside Claude: the shared database, Claude AI and all screens work; the camera, speech recognition and openFDA (outside servers) are not available there, so medication options say so and are not shown — use the web build for those.

## Not done / limits
- AI calls were tested against a stand-in, not the live Claude model; real wording will vary (the guards still apply).
- Sign language: only ASL YES and NO are built in; their rules were tested on synthetic hand data, not on real signers — they need checking (and probably tuning) with deaf signers. Arabic/Egyptian sign language needs a model trained on a licensed ArSL/ESL dataset; until then patients teach their own signs. No continuous signing or sentence translation. The camera is not available inside Claude's preview (the app says so and offers other methods); use the web build. MediaPipe files come from public Git mirrors (see `vendor/mediapipe/NOTICE.md`) because npm/CDN were unreachable from the build environment — replace with the official package and model, pinned by hash, for production. Speech input is disabled inside Claude's preview.
- Blind Mode: gestures and speech were tested with simulated touch events and speech engines in Chromium. They need checking on real iPhones/Android phones with real voices — mobile browsers and screen readers (VoiceOver/TalkBack) can take over swipes and taps, in which case the Blind Mode button and the keyboard shortcut remain. Listening stops while the app speaks (to avoid hearing itself); tap the screen or press Stop to interrupt it.
- Arabic: the interface dictionary covers the patient screens, navigation, accounts, settings and Blind Mode. Doctor-side clinical-support text produced by the rule engine, medical reports and anything people wrote stay in the language they were written in. Egyptian Arabic speech recognition depends on the browser (Chrome supports `ar-EG`; Apple devices use `ar-SA`).
- Natural voice: the Azure route is implemented and tested against a stand-in only; it needs an Azure Speech key to be used.
- No native iOS/Android build. Video calls and push notifications are not implemented.
- Password recovery is impossible by design in cloud mode.
- Clinical engine: warning rules cover a limited set of signs from public NHS pages (not clinical guidelines); the AI parts and openFDA were tested against stand-ins; FDA label text checks are text matches, not a complete interaction database; no licensed drug-interaction service is connected; doctor clinical-support content is English.
- Not clinically validated; no regulatory compliance claimed. The metrics are per-report counts, not accuracy statistics.
